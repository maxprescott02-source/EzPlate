# UX audit, 8 Sep 2026 (production, ezplate-v195)

Method: signed in to production as the owner, read only. Desktop at 1360 x 900 (dark, then light), tablet at 900 x 1180. Every screen and the named modals were opened; nothing was saved. Findings are what a product designer would flag against a Stripe-grade bar. Measurements are from the live DOM, not from screenshots. The naming inversion in CLAUDE.md is deliberate and is not reported. Row ids are U1 to U46 for later reference.

## Verdict

The bones are good: one header grid on every list screen, one row system, real empty and zero-results states, a consistent modal frame, sensible dark and light palettes, and a builder that reads like a receipt. What keeps it short of the bar is not layout, it is consistency of language and of decisions across screens. The same action has five labels (Save, Save changes, Save plate, Add new product, Create menu), the same destructive act has three (Delete, Delete item, Remove), the same product identity renders four ways, and the screen subtitle slot means four different things on four screens. Three flows are longer or blinder than they should be: re-pricing a plate on a menu opens a modal that hides the cost and the suggested price it was opened from; adding an existing plate to a menu lists the 59 plates already on that menu ahead of the 40 that are not; and a missing ingredient in the builder sends the user to another screen instead of creating it in place. On a tablet the builder's Save button and total fall below the fold and the Dashboard silently drops two of its three headline figures. Test fixtures (ZZ-AUDIT plates, a menu named with an HTML tag, a supplier named `<b>ZZ</b>`) are live in production and distort the Dashboard chart to a 380 percent axis. None of it is structural; most of it is a wording or placement pass plus four small flow fixes.

## What is already at the bar

- One header grid on every list screen: title, subtitle, actions on the right, search top left at the same size, and one primary button per screen.
- The row system: Plates, Menu, Products and Ingredients share a 45 px row, muted meta, monospace money, and a right aligned figure.
- Real empty and zero-results states with a recovery link (No plates match, Clear search and filters) on both Menu and Plates.
- The modal frame is one component: same head, same close, same footer order, focus moved on open, Escape closes one layer.
- Money and percentages in the tables are consistent: two decimals, per kg or per unit suffix, one decimal on percents.
- The Menu row verdict pill and the Dashboard KPI share one colour rule anchored to the target.
- The Dashboard scope popover carries its honesty note (ranked by cost efficiency, not earnings) where the ranking is.
- The builder docket is a distinctive, legible metaphor: ingredient over its product, quantity input, unit cost chip, line cost, remove.
- The New ingredient modal disables Save until valid, and the Restore card says Can't be undone.
- Sticky page header with the menu name on Menu, so scope survives scrolling.
- Dark and light themes both hold: no untokenised colour was seen on any screen or modal.
- The privacy disclosure is honest, plain and present at both invoice entry points.

## Flow counts

Counts are from the sidebar, desktop, with the app already open. A click is a click or a keypress that commits.

Cost a new plate: Plates (1), New plate (2), type a name (3), search an ingredient (4), pick it (5), type a quantity (6), repeat 4 to 6 per line, Save plate (7 plus 3 per extra line). After saving the screen stays in the builder and Manage menus appears; the next step (publish) is offered but not named as the next step. If an ingredient does not exist the flow adds Ingredients (1), New ingredient (2), name (3), product search and pick (4, 5), Save (6), Plates (7), reopen the plate (8): eight extra clicks against one if created inline (U3).

Re-price a plate on the menu: Menu (1), row (2), price field (3), Save changes (4). Four clicks, but step 3 is done without seeing the cost or the suggested price (U1). After saving, the row repaints and nothing says what the new food cost is beyond the pill.

Import an invoice: Invoices (1), Upload invoice (2), Drop or browse (3), pick the file (4), review, Apply (5). Or one fewer using the on-page dropzone. After apply, the user is told prices changed but not pointed at the plates whose food cost moved; the Dashboard shows it on the next visit.
