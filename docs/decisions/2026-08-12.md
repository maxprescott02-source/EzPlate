## 1. Where the second button goes on a phone

**The question.** Three screens put two buttons in the phone header when the design allows one: Ingredients ("Set up from products" + "New ingredient"), Products ("Import invoice" + "New product"), Menu ("Existing plate" + "New menu"). You already decided the second one moves somewhere else. This is picking where.

**Why it matters now.** It is worse than a tidiness problem. I measured it: on your phone the **Ingredients header is already wrapping onto two lines at 380px wide**, which is the width everything in the app is built and tested against. It only happens on your data, because "Set up from products" appears when you have unlinked products and your catalogue always does. The test suite cannot see it. So this is a live defect on your phone, not a hypothetical.

**Option A - put it in the row of controls just underneath. (Recommended.)**
Every one of the three screens already has a row of search and filter controls directly below the header. The second button moves into that row on phones only; desktop keeps both buttons where they are.
- Costs: that row reads as "filters", and this puts an action in it. On Products the row already runs to two lines, so it absorbs it; on the other two it may add a line.
- Commits you to: nothing new. This is the pattern the Menu screen already uses - when that screen was rebuilt, its menu switcher was put in exactly this row for exactly this reason.

**Option B - a new one-line strip between the header and the controls, just for actions.**
- Costs: about 44px of vertical space on every one of those screens, every time, even when you are not using the button.
- Commits you to: a new pattern that has to be maintained and applied to any future screen.
- Better than A in one way: it never muddles actions with filters.

**Option C - a "..." menu in the header.**
- I do not recommend this and would push back if you picked it. It takes two taps instead of one, and it needs the floating-dropdown system that is still on the queue as broken. We would build it once now and again later.

**My recommendation: A.** It reuses a decision already made on the Menu screen rather than inventing a fourth answer, it needs no new machinery, and it frees 72-130px, which fixes the wrapping on both affected screens.

**What would change it:** if you look at Ingredients or Products and the action sitting among the filters genuinely reads as a filter to you. That is a judgement about your screens, not something I can measure. If it does, B is the right answer and the extra line is worth paying.

**One more, same question.** Nothing in this app has ever been tested at 360px wide (some Androids, and a few older iPhones in some modes). Should 360 become a width we support and test? I recommend **yes** - after this change it costs nothing, because every header becomes title-plus-one-button and fits easily. Say no and we keep 380 as the floor.

## 2. The restore full-wipe test - your go, and when

**The question.** Do you want to run the last untested step of the disaster-recovery plan, and on what day?

**What it is.** Steps 1 and 2 were done long ago. Step 3 is: wipe the real database completely, then restore it from a backup file, and confirm everything comes back. It is the only part never tried. It was scheduled for "the batch after the redesign finishes", and the redesign finished yesterday, so it is due.

**Why it matters now.** This is the recovery path you would actually use if something went badly wrong. Right now nobody knows whether it works from empty, and the day you need to know is the worst possible day to find out.

**What changed this week.** I rehearsed it on the new staging database yesterday: emptied it, restored into it, and everything came back linked correctly - including the case that once silently broke 76 of 77 dishes. So this is no longer a first attempt. But that was invented data in a different database. What is still untested is your real 412-product export going through the function's 30-second limit, and how the app's loading screen behaves midway through.

**What I would do on the day:** take a fresh export minutes beforehand, save it to your Downloads, rehearse that exact file against staging first, then run it for real. Roughly 15 minutes. If it fails, the export we just took is the way back.

**Options**
- **Yes, and name a day.** Ideally when the café is closed or quiet, so a bad half hour costs nothing.
- **Yes, but only after I show you the rehearsal on staging first.** Slower, and you see it work on invented data before risking real data.
- **No, not yet.** Perfectly reasonable. The cost is that the recovery path stays unproven, and the item keeps sitting at the top of the queue.

**My recommendation: yes, on a closed day.** Not because it is likely to fail, but because the whole value of a backup is knowing it restores, and that is exactly the thing we have never checked.

**What would change it:** if you are about to do anything that matters on the app in the next few days. Then it waits - there is no deadline on this beyond "before other people's data is in there".
