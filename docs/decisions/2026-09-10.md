## 2. May staff delete a product? May staff delete a taught pack?

**Blocks: item 91.** Two sentences, or one if the answer is the same for both.

Measured against `pg_policies` on production, not read off the schema file: **any
member of the café, staff included, can delete any product and any taught pack
today.** Same permissive `FOR ALL` policy that let staff delete your food-cost
history until batch 250 closed it.

The oddity that makes this worth asking rather than assuming: **staff may not
delete a plate** (you decided that in 187) **but may delete every product that
plate costs from.** A deleted product is not recoverable from the app.

⚠️ **`menu_items` is a third such table and is deliberate** — 187 decided staff may
unpublish a dish, and the migration says so. Not part of this question.

- **A — No to both.** Owner-only, using 187's exact idiom with 250 as the worked
  example. **My recommendation**, on the grounds that it matches the decision you
  already made about plates, and a deleted product takes its price history with it.
- **B — No to products, yes to taught packs.** A taught pack is cheap to re-teach;
  a product is not.
- **C — Yes to both.** Leave it as it is. Reasonable if staff genuinely maintain
  the catalogue for you.

**What would change my recommendation:** if staff are the ones who actually keep
the product list tidy, A makes their job impossible and C is right.

## 3. After applying an invoice, should the dialog stay open while it saves?

**Blocks: item 90**, which is otherwise finished — the other three parts shipped in
batches 253 and 254.

Today: the invoice list closes immediately, and a moment later a card tells you what
landed — *"4 of 36 price writes saved — the rest did not reach the server"*. What is
not built is leaving a **refused row on screen, unticked, with its own error**, so you
could fix that one line there and then. That needs the list still open.

⚠️ **I owe you a correction here.** Batch 253 recorded that keeping it open would cost
*"dozens of round trips on café mobile data"*, and I repeated that to you. Measured
against the code rather than reasoned: **the app already waits for those writes** before
it says anything, and they go out in parallel — so it is the slowest of N, not N in
sequence, and it is capped at 15 seconds either way. **The choice does not add waiting.
It only decides whether you are stuck watching a wait that already happens.**

- **A — Leave it.** Screen back immediately, honest count a moment later.
  **My recommendation.** `CLAUDE.md` already says you would rather be told something
  did not save than discover it next week, and you *are* told. This buys per-row
  detail at the cost of blocking you.
- **B — Keep it open.** You wait on the invoice list, and any refused row stays put
  with its error so you can retry it.

**What would change my recommendation:** whether you would actually act on a refused
row on the spot. If you would just re-import the invoice, A is right and B is a wait
for nothing. If you would fix the one line, B pays for itself.
