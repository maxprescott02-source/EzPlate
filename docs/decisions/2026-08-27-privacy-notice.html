Privacy notice

EzPlate sends some of your data to Google to be read by an AI. This is what goes, and what happens to it.

What leaves this app

Two things, and nothing else:

  • Invoice text. When you import a supplier invoice, the text of that invoice is sent to Google so a second reader can check the line items the app read. That includes your supplier’s name, the products and the prices on it.
  • Plate names and costing figures. When the Dashboard writes an insight, the plate names and the numbers behind them are sent to Google to be phrased into a sentence.

Your login, your password and your customers’ details are never sent anywhere but the database that runs the app.

What Google does with it

EzPlate uses Google’s free tier of the Gemini API. Under Google’s terms for that tier:

  • Google may use what is sent to improve its products — which includes training its models.
  • Human reviewers at Google may read it.

That is unusual and it is why this notice exists rather than a line in a longer document. If your supplier pricing is commercially sensitive to you, this is the thing to weigh.

What you can turn off

  • Dashboard insights. Settings › AI suggestions. Off means no plate names and no figures are sent, and nothing is computed.
  • The invoice second reader. It only runs when you import an invoice. Not importing sends nothing.

Who runs this

EzPlate is run by Scoopy’s Family Cafe. There is no analytics and no tracking of any kind in this app, and nothing is sold or shared with anyone else.

If this changes — and it is intended to, by moving to a paid tier Google may not train on — this notice changes with it and you will be asked to read it again.

Why it is worded this specifically. The item's own rule is that vague wording does not discharge a disclosure — “we may share data with service providers” is exactly the phrasing that hides the material fact. Naming Google, naming the free tier, and saying plainly that submissions may be used for training and that humans may read them is what makes this a disclosure rather than a notice. Those are Google's published terms for that tier, not a worst case.

What changes when item 2b ships. On the paid tier Google “doesn't use your prompts or responses to improve our products”. The second section then says the opposite and the screens do not move — the acceptance, the link placements and the restatement at import all stay.

If you want it shorter, say which section to cut and I will cut it — but the two bullets under “What Google does with it” are the ones that make it worth showing at all.
